# MONARQUE: CARDS OF THE FALLEN
## Complete Story & Gameplay Design Specification

*A universe born from the fading of a primordial consciousness. A king terrified of oblivion. Thirty-two mythological fighters forced into a tournament. Eighty imprisoned supernatural beings transformed into living weapons. And a champion unknowingly being forged into the vessel of the very being who intends to take control of their existence.*

---

# 1. The Origin of Existence

Before creation, there was no light or darkness, no time or space, no life or death. There was only an immense consciousness suspended in absolute nothingness.

It possessed unimaginable power but had no understanding of what it was, why it existed, or whether anything had ever existed before it.

This consciousness never named itself. Later civilizations would come to call it **AION**.

For an eternity, AION existed alone. With nothing to experience, nothing to challenge it, and nothing to give its existence meaning, its consciousness eventually began to fade.

As that consciousness deteriorated, the immeasurable power contained within it erupted in a cataclysmic event known as **the Outburst**.

The Outburst created the universe, the laws governing existence, and every form of life: gods, demons, monsters, humans, and countless other beings across innumerable worlds.

The power released by AION became known as **Essence**, the fundamental source of supernatural power throughout existence.

## 1.1 The Three Principles of Essence

Essence governs the power, survival, and evolution of every supernatural being. Three distinct concepts determine how it operates.

**Total Essence**

The total quantity of Essence a being possesses. It represents the overall amount of supernatural power contained within that being.

**Essence concentration**

The concentration of that power within a being. Higher concentration produces greater potential power, but excessive concentration places increasing strain on the consciousness that contains it.

**Containment capacity**

The amount and intensity of Essence a being's body, soul, and consciousness can safely contain and channel without losing stability or identity.

These concepts are related, but they are not interchangeable. A being can possess extraordinary total Essence yet lack the capacity to withstand it. Another being may possess less total Essence but have a greater ability to contain and control the power it possesses.

## 1.2 The Law of Essence

The universe follows an immutable law:

- Greater Essence concentration provides greater supernatural power.
- Every consciousness has a limit to how much Essence it can safely contain.
- When concentration exceeds that limit, the consciousness begins to deteriorate.
- The greater the excess, the faster the consciousness fades.
- Containment capacity can evolve through sustained exposure to supernatural strain, but it never removes the Law of Essence itself.

A being may become powerful enough to challenge the laws of existence and still be destroyed by the power that makes it extraordinary.

No amount of adaptation grants absolute immunity. Every vessel retains a limit.

This law explains the rise of Monarque, the purpose of his tournament, and the possibility of his eventual downfall.

---

# 2. Monarque — The King Who Fears Oblivion

Among all beings born from the Outburst, one rose above the rest.

**Monarque.**

His Essence concentration is nearly four times that of the strongest being beneath him. Gods, demons, ancient monsters, and legendary warriors feared throughout existence are insignificant before his might.

No conventional army can threaten him. No ordinary opponent can challenge him. For ages, he has existed beyond the reach of those who might oppose him.

But even Monarque cannot escape the Law of Essence.

His extraordinary Essence concentration begins to destroy the consciousness that controls it. Memories disappear. Thoughts fragment. His sense of identity becomes increasingly unstable.

He recognizes the same fate that befell AION approaching him.

Eventually, his consciousness will fade, leaving his immense power behind.

For a being who has conquered almost everything, the possibility of ceasing to exist is intolerable.

## 2.1 Why Monarque Cannot Simply Take Another Body

Monarque searches for a way to transfer his consciousness into another being.

He tests the possibility of possessing powerful gods, demons, ancient monsters, and other extraordinary entities. Yet every potential vessel fails.

Some are destroyed immediately. Others collapse as their consciousness is overwhelmed by the concentration of Essence forced into them.

He discovers that raw strength alone is insufficient.

A vessel must possess the capacity to contain Monarque's Essence while preserving its own independent consciousness. Otherwise, the body may survive briefly, but the identity required to control the power will deteriorate.

This capacity cannot be transferred from one being to another. It cannot be manufactured instantly, nor can it be obtained through strength alone.

It must emerge through sustained adaptation to extraordinary supernatural strain.

Monarque cannot simply choose the strongest being in existence and take control. He must create the conditions under which a suitable vessel can develop.

He reaches a terrible conclusion.

**If no suitable vessel exists, he will create one.**

## 2.2 The Experiment

Monarque devises a plan to force a candidate through repeated encounters with different supernatural powers and increasingly dangerous opponents.

He needs beings capable of surviving these trials, entities whose powers can expose the candidates to unfamiliar supernatural natures, and a controlled environment in which the results can be observed.

This requires two distinct collections:

- Thirty-two potential vessel candidates who will compete in the tournament.
- Eighty imprisoned supernatural beings whose powers will become the instruments of the experiment.

The tournament is not merely entertainment or a contest for a wish.

It is Monarque's attempt to manufacture a vessel capable of containing his consciousness and Essence.

The champion is his intended body.

---

# 3. The Collection — Thirty-Two Fighters and Eighty Bound Beings

Monarque travels across the universe, moving between mythological realms, divine kingdoms, demon domains, forgotten dimensions, and worlds inhabited by supernatural entities.

He defeats and humiliates the most powerful gods, demons, monsters, and legendary warriors he encounters.

From these worlds, he assembles two collections with different purposes.

## 3.1 The Thirty-Two Tournament Fighters

Monarque selects **32 formidable fighters** whose bodies, souls, and consciousnesses possess the potential to survive the tournament and adapt under extraordinary supernatural pressure.

Their origins include:

- Greek mythology
- Japanese mythology
- Norse mythology
- Egyptian mythology
- Yokai and other supernatural beings
- Demons and devils
- Ancient monsters and legendary warriors

These categories can overlap. A being may originate from a particular mythology while also belonging to a broader category of supernatural entities.

The defining requirement is not simply strength. Monarque needs candidates whose consciousness remains stable enough to endure the tournament and whose containment capacity might evolve through repeated battles.

They are not necessarily the strongest beings in existence. They are the most promising candidates for his experiment.

Monarque transports them to a realm beyond the reach of their original worlds and announces a knockout tournament.

He promises that the ultimate victor will regain their freedom and continue their existence.

Every defeated fighter, he declares, will have their soul bound into a card, becoming a servant of the victor.

The contestants fight for survival, pride, revenge, and the possibility of reclaiming their destiny.

None understands the true purpose of the tournament.

## 3.2 The Eighty Bound Beings

The second collection consists of **80 additional supernatural beings**.

They originate from the same mythological traditions and supernatural worlds as the tournament fighters. They include gods, spirits, yokai, demons, devils, monsters, and other extraordinary entities.

These beings are not selected to compete in the main tournament bracket.

Instead, Monarque identifies them as suitable for a different purpose: their consciousness can remain sufficiently stable when bound and compressed into a supernatural vessel.

Through an ancient binding process, Monarque seals each being into a combat card.

These are not ordinary cards containing illustrations or representations of abilities.

**Every card contains an actual imprisoned entity.**

The being's identity, supernatural nature, and powers remain bound within the card. Its consciousness persists inside the prison, aware of its captivity but unable to escape Monarque's authority.

The result is a collection of **80 playable character cards**.

Each card represents a unique supernatural being whose power can be invoked by a tournament fighter.

Some possess elemental powers. Others manipulate space, time, curses, illusions, souls, physical laws, or other supernatural forces. Their abilities differ according to their identities and natures.

The 80 beings are more than weapons. They are the living instruments of Monarque's experiment, each exposing the fighters to a different form of supernatural power.

Their powers are extraordinary, but invoking them carries a price.

---

# 4. The True Purpose of the Tournament

Monarque presents the tournament as a competition in which the strongest warrior earns freedom.

The truth is far more sinister.

The tournament is an experiment designed to manufacture a vessel capable of containing his consciousness and Essence.

Monarque believes that repeated battles against exceptional opponents will force the champion to evolve beyond the limitations of every existing being.

The 32 fighters are necessary because Monarque cannot know in advance which candidate will adapt successfully.

The 80 bound beings are necessary because a single opponent or a single supernatural power cannot provide the diversity of strain required by his experiment.

Each bound-being card introduces a different supernatural nature. By invoking these powers, the fighters expose their bodies, souls, and consciousnesses to forces they were never naturally designed to contain.

The experiment is dangerous.

Some fighters struggle to control the powers they invoke. Others suffer severe spiritual strain. Some become unstable when incompatible supernatural forces begin to overwhelm their identities.

Monarque observes every result.

He is searching for a being capable of wielding many different supernatural powers while maintaining a stable, independent consciousness.

The champion is not merely supposed to become the strongest fighter.

They must become a vessel that can contain Monarque without immediately collapsing or losing their identity.

Monarque believes the tournament will produce precisely that being.

He does not understand that the experiment may create someone capable of resisting him.

# 5. Soul Consumption — The Price of Supernatural Power

Soul Consumption is the central resource system of Story Mode. It replaces Ki as the primary cost for invoking the 80 bound-being cards.

Regular Battle Mode retains its existing Ki system and mechanics.

## 5.1 Why Invoking a Card Consumes the Soul

The 80 combat cards contain real supernatural entities.

When a fighter invokes a card, the imprisoned being's power passes through the fighter's body and soul. The card allows the fighter to wield an ability they do not naturally possess.

However, a consciousness cannot channel an unfamiliar supernatural nature without consequence.

The invocation consumes part of the user's available soul reserve.

This is not simply physical energy or magical stamina. It represents the spiritual cost of forcing another being's supernatural nature through one's own existence.

The greater the strain, the greater the Soul Consumption.

Every invocation therefore creates a decision: how much of the fighter's available soul should be sacrificed for an immediate tactical advantage?

## 5.2 Individual Soul Consumption Costs

Each of the 80 bound-being cards has its own Soul Consumption value.

The cost depends on several factors:

- **Power:** More formidable entities generally require greater sacrifice.
- **Nature:** Certain supernatural abilities are inherently difficult to channel.
- **Stability:** Entities whose powers resist control or destabilize the user may demand a higher cost.
- **Complexity:** Abilities that manipulate fundamental laws or multiple supernatural forces may require more of the user's soul.

Raw strength is not the only factor. A relatively weak entity with an unstable or incompatible nature may be more costly to invoke than a stronger but more cooperative one.

The exact values are defined during gameplay balancing.

## 5.3 How Invocation Works

When a fighter activates a bound-being card:

1. **Invocation:** The fighter activates the card.
2. **Release:** The imprisoned entity's supernatural power manifests.
3. **Consumption:** The fighter pays the card's individual Soul Consumption cost.
4. **Resolution:** The ability resolves through the existing card-combat rules.
5. **Aftermath:** The fighter must account for their remaining soul reserve before invoking further powers.

A powerful invocation may turn a losing duel around, but using it carelessly can leave the fighter unable to afford a crucial ability later.

The player must consider not only which ability is strongest, but also whether its immediate benefit justifies its cost.

## 5.4 Soul Consumption and Lasting Consequences

For gameplay purposes, Soul Consumption is the resource spent to invoke bound-being cards. It does not automatically mean that every invocation permanently destroys a piece of the character's soul.

The recommended design is to let ordinary consumption reduce the fighter's available soul reserve during combat, while excessive use or particular dangerous cards can produce lasting consequences.

This preserves meaningful resource management without requiring every card activation to permanently destroy the player character.

The Law of Essence remains separate from the Soul Consumption resource system. Spending soul reserve limits how frequently a fighter can invoke supernatural powers; containment capacity determines how well the fighter's consciousness can withstand Essence and supernatural strain.

The two systems are connected through the story, but they are not identical.

## 5.5 Soul Consumption and Fighter Evolution

The champion's ability to withstand supernatural strain gradually improves throughout the tournament.

Repeated exposure to different supernatural natures allows the champion to develop greater containment capacity. Powers that would previously have caused severe instability become more manageable.

However, evolution does not make the champion immune to the Law of Essence, nor does it automatically eliminate Soul Consumption costs.

The champion can withstand more than before, but every invocation remains a strategic decision.

# 6. Story Mode Gameplay — The Existing Card Battle System

Story Mode retains the existing Battle Mode card-combat engine as its foundation.

The mythology, card identities, progression, and resource economy change. The core duel structure remains recognizable.

**Regular Battle Mode remains unchanged.**

## 6.1 The Duel

Each duel is a one-on-one battle.

- Each fighter begins with 100 HP.
- Each fighter receives a 15-card opening hand under the existing opening-hand rules, including Powerful cards and two special Last Stand cards.
- Combat cards retain their Power, Rank, tier, and associated gameplay properties.
- The leader secretly chooses whether the round will be decided by POWER or RANK and commits a card.
- The opponent responds without knowing the leader's chosen condition.
- Cards are revealed, abilities and modifiers resolve, and the round winner is determined.
- HP and other applicable combat resources change according to the duel's placement rules.

The winner is not necessarily the fighter who commits the strongest card.

Prediction, bluffing, resource management, card selection, and timing all matter.

## 6.2 Power and Rank

The leader chooses between two competing conditions:

**POWER:** The higher Power value wins.

**RANK:** The lower Rank number wins, with Rank 1 being the strongest rank.

The leader commits a card while concealing the chosen condition. The opponent must select a response without knowing which condition will decide the round.

This creates a tactical guessing game in which the value of a card depends on both its properties and the opponent's likely decision.

## 6.3 Card Tiers and Combat Costs

Cards retain their existing Power, Rank, and tier structure.

| Tier | Power range | Existing Battle Mode Ki cost |
|---|---:|---:|
| OP | 990 and above | 5 Ki |
| Powerful | 880–989 | 3 Ki |
| Normal | 100–879 | 1 Ki |
| Weak | Below 100 | Last Stand pool |

These are the existing Battle Mode classifications and Ki costs. Story Mode must define its Soul Consumption values independently rather than converting Ki costs directly into soul costs.

The 80 bound-being cards have their own individual Soul Consumption values based on their abilities and supernatural characteristics.

## 6.4 Boosts, Cooldowns, and Round Effects

The existing combat structure includes:

- Power and Rank boosts.
- Card-tier restrictions on boosting.
- Ability cooldowns.
- Health and resource changes after rounds.
- Last Stand cards and their existing restrictions.
- Arena Pressure and other round effects.
- Resource-related actions such as Charge Ki in the existing Battle Mode.

Story Mode should preserve the combat logic wherever possible, adapting resource-dependent actions to the Soul Consumption system where necessary.

For example, a Story Mode ability that previously required Ki should specify its Soul Consumption cost or another clearly defined restriction.

Last Stand and other elimination rules must remain consistent with the existing duel design unless deliberately revised.

## 6.5 Fighter Signature Abilities and Bound-Being Cards

The 32 tournament fighters have their own identities and signature abilities.

The 80 bound-being cards provide additional supernatural powers that fighters can invoke during combat.

These are distinct gameplay elements:

- **Fighter signature abilities** express the individual nature and fighting style of a tournament contestant.
- **Bound-being cards** allow the contestant to invoke the supernatural power of an imprisoned entity.

The existing ability framework governs timing, reactions, passive effects, cooldowns, and restrictions. Story Mode must define how signature abilities interact with Soul Consumption and the 80 cards without replacing the existing battle loop.

Fighters do not use ordinary Wild Cards in Story Mode. Monarque alone retains his Game Master interventions, adapted to the one-on-one format.

His interventions should be telegraphed and counterable, ensuring that he remains dangerous without making combat arbitrary.

## 6.6 Story Mode Resource Economy

Story Mode removes the ordinary shop, coins, and regular Draw action. The opening hand and tournament progression provide the core structure.

Soul Consumption replaces Ki as the primary cost for invoking bound-being cards. The player's available soul reserve limits how frequently and how powerfully they can invoke the imprisoned entities.

This creates a different resource-management challenge from Battle Mode.

A fighter must decide not only which card is likely to win a round, but also whether its supernatural power is worth the amount of soul it consumes.

# 7. Tournament Structure and Progression

The tournament begins with 32 fighters and follows a knockout bracket.

The player's matches are played interactively. The other bracket matches are simulated behind the scenes.

| Stage | Story and gameplay |
|---|---|
| Round 1 — Round of 32 | The champion begins the tournament and encounters the first opponent. |
| Round 2 — Round of 16 | Surviving fighters continue with their accumulated powers and resources. |
| Round 3 — Quarter-finals | The champion faces increasingly dangerous opponents. |
| Round 4 — Semi-finals | Only the strongest contenders remain. |
| Round 5 — Tournament Final | The champion defeats the final bracket opponent. |
| Round 6 — The Berserker | Monarque lifts a sealed warrior the champion has never faced out of the archive and pours a third of his power into them. |
| Final — Monarque | The champion confronts the true architect of the tournament. |

The first five stages form the tournament bracket. The sixth is a special encounter after the bracket final, followed by the confrontation with Monarque.

## 7.1 Rewards After Victory

After defeating an opponent, the champion chooses one of two rewards:

1. Claim the defeated fighter's signature-power card.
2. Claim one of that fighter's previously collected power cards instead.

The selected reward changes the champion's future combat options and progression.

The champion's collection grows through these choices, while the 80 bound-being cards provide the supernatural powers used during the duels.

This distinction keeps tournament progression and combat-card invocation connected without making the 32 fighters and 80 bound beings the same collection.

---

# 8. The Champion Evolves

Every victory brings the champion closer to Monarque's intended design.

The champion acquires powers, learns to channel unfamiliar supernatural forces, and survives increasingly dangerous opponents.

The 80 bound-being cards expose the champion to a broad range of supernatural natures. Each invocation tests the limits of their soul reserve and their ability to maintain control over foreign power.

Their body, soul, and consciousness gradually adapt to the accumulated strain.

This evolution must be foreshadowed throughout the tournament rather than introduced only before the final battle.

## 8.1 Early Tournament — Resistance

At first, invoking unfamiliar supernatural powers places significant strain on the champion.

Certain powerful invocations produce signs of instability. The champion must manage their soul reserve carefully and cannot safely wield every power without consequence.

These early struggles establish the limits the champion must overcome.

## 8.2 Middle Rounds — Adaptation

As the champion survives additional battles, their containment capacity begins to improve.

They become more capable of maintaining control while channeling unfamiliar supernatural natures. Powers that once caused severe instability become more manageable, although their Soul Consumption costs remain relevant.

The champion is not immune to the Law of Essence. They are learning to withstand more than they could before.

## 8.3 Tournament Final — Unusual Stability

By the tournament final, Monarque begins noticing something extraordinary.

The champion can channel multiple incompatible supernatural powers while maintaining a more stable consciousness than Monarque expected.

He interprets this as confirmation that his experiment is succeeding.

He believes he has finally created a suitable vessel.

But the champion's growing stability has another consequence: they are becoming an independent being capable of resisting the consciousness Monarque intends to impose upon them.

# 9. Round 6 — The Berserker

*This section reflects the game as built. The earlier "Corrupted" warrior (a resurrected, defeated opponent) was replaced by the Berserker; the screenplay in `docs/STORY_SCREENPLAY.md` is canonical.*

After the champion wins the tournament final, Monarque reveals his last test. Far below the arena, in the archive of the bound, a single seal cracks.

He drags out a warrior the champion has **never faced** and whose power the champion does not hold. The warrior is chosen at random from that unseen pool, so it is a different fighter from run to run.

Monarque pours **a third of his own power** into them. The warrior's body cannot contain it. Their mind gives way and only the rage remains.

In the duel the Berserker has the following rules:
- **+50% HP, Soul and power effects** (150 HP, 21 opening Soul, Soul cap 30; effects x1.5, Step III). Costs the wielder pays are not scaled.
- Their own power plus five more drawn from fighters the champion has not beaten, and one Edict of Monarque's own.
- Round 6 is the hardest bracket fight and tests flawless card play.

Monarque expects the Berserker to break the champion and leave behind the perfect vessel. He has underestimated the champion's evolution: the champion has adapted to every power in the tournament and survives what destroys the Berserker.

## 9.1 The Power Taken

When the Berserker falls, the surge burns out of them and Monarque is shaken.

The champion may take **only the Berserker's exact power**. It is an ordinary collected power with one difference: when it is played it restores 40% of the champion's maximum HP and Soul on top of its own effect, and it keeps its +50% in the final bouts. It cannot be kept as a keepsake for later runs.

Monarque never grants the champion a power. Every power the champion holds comes from a being the champion beat or from the Berserker.

The gap between the champion and Monarque has never been smaller.

---

# 10. The Final Battle — The Fall of Monarque

Monarque enters the arena himself.

His deception is no longer necessary. The champion has become the vessel he intended to claim, and he moves to take control of it.

The final battle uses the existing card-combat foundation, with Monarque's overwhelming power represented through the Game Master mechanics and his special interventions.

The champion possesses the accumulated strength of the tournament, the abilities acquired through progression, a vessel hardened by repeated supernatural strain, and a fraction of Monarque's own Essence.

Monarque unleashes his full might, confident that no being can overcome him.

But his consciousness continues to falter under the strain of his extraordinary Essence concentration. His obsession with escaping oblivion has blinded him to the possibility that his creation might become his greatest opponent.

The battle pushes both beings to their limits.

Monarque attempts to overwhelm the champion and take control of their identity. The champion resists, adapts, and continues fighting.

The champion's evolved containment capacity makes survival possible, but it does not remove the Law of Essence. Monarque remains extraordinarily powerful, and the champion must still overcome him through the battle.

As the confrontation continues, Monarque's deteriorating consciousness becomes an increasingly dangerous weakness.

At last, the champion defeats him.

Monarque's attempt to escape his own inevitable end has created the very being capable of ending his reign.

---

# 11. The Ending — The Freedom of the Fallen

Monarque's defeat collapses the authority sustaining his bindings.

Every card created by Monarque is sustained by his authority over Essence. The physical card is merely the vessel for a seal whose power originates from him.

The cards do not need to be in Monarque's possession to remain active. Their bindings continue wherever they are held, carried, or used because the authority sustaining their prisons remains in effect.

When Monarque is defeated, that authority collapses.

Across the universe, the seals begin to fail. The cards crack and shatter, unable to sustain their imprisoned entities without the power that created their bindings.

The curse holding the defeated fighters and the 80 bound beings in their cards comes undone.

The imprisoned souls are released. The entities bound within the combat cards regain their freedom, while the tournament fighters are freed from Monarque's control.

Gods return to their realms. Demons return to their domains. Yokai, monsters, spirits, and other supernatural beings return to the worlds from which they were taken.

The tournament is over.

The fallen are free.

The champion has accomplished what no being before them has achieved: defeating the most powerful being born from the Outburst.

The 80 cards were instruments of imprisonment and power, but their destruction becomes the means by which their captives finally regain their freedom.

The universe continues without Monarque's rule.

# 12. Core Design Principles

## 12.1 Story

A primordial consciousness gives rise to existence. Monarque, the most powerful being born from the Outburst, fears the fading of his own consciousness and creates a tournament to manufacture a vessel capable of containing him.

## 12.2 The Law of Essence

Total Essence, Essence concentration, and containment capacity are distinct concepts.

Monarque's immense power threatens his consciousness because his concentration exceeds what he can safely contain. The champion's containment capacity gradually evolves through sustained exposure to different supernatural natures, but the Law of Essence itself never disappears.

## 12.3 The Thirty-Two Tournament Fighters

Thirty-two mythological and supernatural beings are selected for their potential to endure the tournament and evolve into a suitable vessel. Their battles form the main story progression.

## 12.4 The Eighty Bound-Being Cards

Eighty additional supernatural beings are imprisoned within combat cards. Their individual identities and powers give the tournament fighters access to extraordinary abilities.

Each card has its own Soul Consumption cost. The cards are actual prisons containing supernatural entities, not merely representations of abilities.

## 12.5 Soul Consumption

Story Mode replaces Ki as the primary cost for invoking bound-being cards. Fighters must manage their soul reserves carefully, deciding when the power of an entity is worth the spiritual cost.

Soul Consumption is a gameplay resource; containment capacity determines how well a consciousness withstands Essence and supernatural strain.

Regular Battle Mode retains its existing Ki system.

## 12.6 Combat

Story Mode preserves the existing one-on-one card-battle structure: HP, POWER/RANK decisions, card tiers, boosts, cooldowns, signature abilities, and round resolution.

Resource-dependent mechanics are adapted to Soul Consumption where required, without replacing the established battle loop.

## 12.7 Progression

After victories, the champion chooses powers from defeated fighters. The champion's collection and growing ability to withstand supernatural strain represent the evolution of the intended vessel.

The champion's increased containment capacity must be foreshadowed throughout the tournament.

## 12.8 The Berserker

The Berserker is a warrior from the archive that the champion has never faced. Monarque pours a third of his power into them (+50% HP, Soul and power effects) and their mind gives way.

When they fall, the champion may take only their exact power. It restores 40% of maximum HP and Soul when played and is the bridge to the final battle.

## 12.9 The Finale

The champion's evolved vessel makes the final battle possible, but does not grant immunity to the Law of Essence.

Monarque's defeat collapses his authority over Essence, causing the binding seals to fail and releasing the imprisoned fighters and all eighty bound beings.

# The Central Theme

**The obsession with escaping mortality can become the very weakness that brings about one's destruction.**

Monarque believes that power can overcome the limits of existence. He captures thirty-two fighters to create a perfect vessel and imprisons eighty supernatural beings to turn their powers into weapons.

He believes he can control the process, determine the outcome, and eventually escape the fate that destroyed AION's consciousness.

But the experiment changes the champion in ways Monarque cannot fully control.

The repeated battles, the supernatural powers, and the strain imposed by the bound beings create a vessel capable of resisting him. The power taken from the fallen Berserker, forged from a third of Monarque's own strength, lets the champion stand against him, making the final confrontation possible.

In *Monarque: Cards of the Fallen*, every victory advances the champion's evolution, every bound card represents an imprisoned consciousness, and every invocation carries a price.

The tournament is not simply a contest to determine the strongest fighter.

It is an experiment in the limits of consciousness, the cost of power, and the danger of trying to escape the laws that govern existence.

Monarque creates the being capable of defeating him—and his defeat finally frees the souls he sought to control.
